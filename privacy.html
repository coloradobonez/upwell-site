Privacy policy

Effective November 26, 2026 (updated the same day to cover lives, replays, Twitch, YouTube Analytics, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, and more AI choices, and on November 27, 2026 to describe the sign-in helper, how long YouTube data is kept, and the update check)

Upwell is a desktop app that runs on your own computer. This policy explains what it does with your information. The short version: your data stays on your computer, and Upwell only sends something somewhere when you ask it to.

What stays on your computer

  • Your videos. Upwell reads them where they are and never changes the originals.
  • Your library: video details, titles, descriptions, tags, notes, exports, and upload history, kept in a local database in your Windows user folder.
  • Channel statistics, whether you import them from a YouTube Studio export file or Upwell reads them from YouTube Analytics.
  • Sign-in credentials (your Google sign-in token and any API key you add), kept in Windows Credential Manager on your computer.

The developer never receives your videos, your library, your credentials, or any usage data. Upwell has no analytics, ads, or tracking.

The sign-in helper

TikTok and Meta only complete a sign-in when the app's secret key is included, and a secret can't be kept safe inside a desktop app. So when you connect TikTok, Instagram, or Facebook with Upwell's built-in connection, a small sign-in helper run for Upwell (hosted on Cloudflare) adds that secret:

  • The one-time sign-in code from TikTok or Facebook goes from your computer to the helper, which adds the secret, sends it on to TikTok or Meta, and passes their answer (your sign-in token) straight back to your computer. For TikTok, renewing the sign-in and disconnecting go the same way.
  • The helper stores nothing: no codes, tokens, accounts, or logs of what passes through. Your Facebook Page token never passes through it; Upwell gets that from Facebook directly.
  • Like any website, Cloudflare sees your computer's network address when the helper is used; the helper uses it only to limit repeated attempts from one network.
  • Your videos, captions, and library never go through it. Posting goes straight from your computer to TikTok, Instagram, or Facebook.

If you set up your own TikTok or Meta developer app in Upwell's settings instead, the helper isn't used at all. YouTube and Twitch don't need it.

Google and YouTube data

Upwell uses YouTube API Services. If you connect a YouTube channel, Upwell asks Google for four permissions:

  • Upload videos (youtube.upload): to upload the video file, title, description, tags, settings, and thumbnail you choose, when you press Upload.
  • View your YouTube account (youtube.readonly): to show which channel is connected (its name and handle), list your channel's videos, and read their public view, like, and comment counts.
  • Manage your YouTube account (youtube.force-ssl): to schedule the lives you plan in Upwell, connect them to your stream key, set their title, description, tags, category, and thumbnail, update a replay's details and chapters after a stream, and read your stream keys' names so you can pick one. Upwell only changes what you ask it to, when you press Schedule, Save, or Update, and only on the live or video you chose.
  • View YouTube Analytics reports (yt-analytics.readonly): to read your videos' statistics (views, watch time, thumbnail impressions and click-through, likes, comments, shares, subscribers, and live viewers) for Channel insights and each video's Results. This is read-only.

Upwell never deletes anything on YouTube, even though the "manage" permission technically allows it. It can't access anything else in your Google account, such as Gmail or Drive. The information Google returns (your channel's name, your stream keys' names, the IDs of the videos and lives you publish, your videos' titles, descriptions, tags, and publish times, and their statistics) is stored only on your computer and used only to show you what's connected, link to what you've published, and learn what works on your channel (the title score, the best time to post, and tag suggestions). Upwell never stores or shows your stream key itself. None of it is sold, shared, or used for advertising.

How long it's kept. Upwell refreshes this information from YouTube when you open it. Videos YouTube no longer returns (for example, ones you deleted) are removed from Upwell after 30 days. When you disconnect YouTube in Upwell, everything Upwell got from YouTube is deleted from your computer right away. If you remove Upwell's access on Google's side instead, or don't open Upwell for a while, it's deleted 30 days after the last successful refresh. Numbers you type in yourself and Upwell's own records of what you published stay until you remove them.

Upwell's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Uploads are also subject to the YouTube Terms of Service and Google's Privacy Policy.

Twitch data

If you connect a Twitch channel, Upwell signs in with Twitch's device code flow (you approve a short code on twitch.tv) and asks for two permissions:

  • Manage your broadcast (channel:manage:broadcast): to set your stream's title, category, and tags when you press Set on Twitch.
  • Manage your schedule (channel:manage:schedule): to add a stream you plan in Upwell to your Twitch schedule when you press Add to schedule.

Upwell also reads your channel's name and your past broadcasts' titles, dates, lengths, and view counts to show them in Channel insights. The sign-in token stays in Windows Credential Manager on your computer, and the information is stored only there. Nothing is sold, shared, or used for advertising. You can disconnect in Upwell's settings or in your Twitch connections.

TikTok data

If you connect a TikTok account, Upwell signs in with TikTok's Login Kit (in your browser) and asks for three permissions:

  • Basic profile (user.info.basic): to show which account is connected.
  • Post to TikTok (video.publish): to post the video you choose, with the caption, audience, and settings you pick, when you press Post to TikTok.
  • Upload to drafts (video.upload): to send the video you choose to your TikTok drafts when you press Send to TikTok drafts.

Upwell reads your account's nickname, username, and posting options (such as who can watch and whether comments, Duet, and Stitch are allowed) each time you open the TikTok post form, and keeps the IDs of the posts it made. The sign-in token stays in Windows Credential Manager on your computer, and the information is stored only there. With Upwell's built-in connection, signing in, renewing, and disconnecting pass through the sign-in helper described above, which stores nothing. Nothing is sold, shared, or used for advertising. You can disconnect in Upwell's settings, which revokes Upwell's access, or under Security and permissions in the TikTok app.

Instagram and Facebook data

If you connect Instagram and Facebook, Upwell signs in with Facebook Login and asks for permission to see the Facebook Pages you manage and their linked Instagram professional accounts (pages_show_list, pages_read_engagement, business_management, instagram_basic), to post Reels to that Instagram account (instagram_content_publish), and to post Reels to your Page (pages_manage_posts). Upwell posts only the video and caption you choose, when you press Post and confirm.

Upwell keeps the name and ID of the Page you pick, its Instagram username and ID, and the IDs and links of the Reels it posted. The Page token stays in Windows Credential Manager on your computer, and the information is stored only there. With Upwell's built-in connection, the one-time sign-in code passes through the sign-in helper described above, which stores nothing; the Page token never does. Nothing is sold, shared, or used for advertising. You can disconnect in Upwell's settings, and remove Upwell's access in your Facebook settings under Business Integrations or in Instagram's Accounts Center.

Optional AI writing help

Upwell's free writer runs entirely on your computer and sends nothing. If you add your own API key for Claude (Anthropic), ChatGPT (OpenAI), or Gemini (Google) and ask it to write, Upwell sends that company the text details you entered about the video (for example the subject, style, and notes), your brand's writing guidelines, and example titles, to draft a title and description. It doesn't send your video files. Each request is covered by that company's own privacy policy: Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google. If you use a local AI (Ollama or LM Studio), the request stays on your computer. If you don't add a key or choose a local AI, nothing is sent.

Checking for updates

Once a day, Upwell downloads a small file from its website to see whether a newer version is out, and tells you if one is. Nothing about you or your videos is sent; like any website, the site's host sees your computer's network address. You can turn this off in Settings → About.

Removing your data

  • Disconnect YouTube in Upwell's settings. This revokes Upwell's access, deletes the stored sign-in token, and deletes the channel's videos, statistics, and insights Upwell got from YouTube. You can also remove access at myaccount.google.com/connections.
  • Disconnect TikTok, Instagram and Facebook, or Twitch in Upwell's settings. This deletes the stored sign-in tokens from your computer.
  • Delete everything by uninstalling Upwell and removing its data folder (%APPDATA%\app.upwell.desktop). Your original videos are never touched.
